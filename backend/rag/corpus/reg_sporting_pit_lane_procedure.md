title: Pit lane procedure — entry, exit, and unsafe release
source: FIA Formula 1 Sporting Regulations 2024, Articles 34 and 28
section: 34 — Pit lane procedure
topics: [pit-lane, unsafe-release, pit-exit, speed-limit, controversy, penalty]
---

Article 34.1 defines the pit lane as the area immediately behind the pit wall. The speed limit in the pit lane is 80 km/h during a race (60 km/h during practice and qualifying). Article 34.13 states that it is the responsibility of the team to release a car only when it is safe to do so and only when the driver is properly secured. An unsafe release — where a car is released into the path of another car — is penalised under Art 34.14 with a minimum drive-through penalty, upgradeable to a time penalty or stop-go at the stewards' discretion. Article 28.2 covers the pit lane entry, requiring drivers to not cross the white line at the pit lane entrance unless signalled by marshals. Unsafe releases are one of the most litigated areas of F1 stewarding because the definition of "unsafe" is subjective. Teams argue a release is only unsafe if actual contact occurred or was unavoidable. The FIA Technical Delegate reviews onboard footage to determine if the mechanic had a clear view of the pit lane before releasing the car.
