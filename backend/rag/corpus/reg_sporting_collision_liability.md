The FIA Driving Standards Guidelines provide the framework stewards use to assign fault in collisions. Key principles: (1) A driver who is predominantly alongside another car entering a corner has a right to be there and the other driver must leave space; (2) A driver who is only partially alongside (front wing alongside rear wheels) at the apex does not have priority; (3) On the straight, a driver may not change direction when another car is already committed to a move. "Racing incident" is the determination used when fault cannot be clearly assigned to one driver — no penalty is issued but it is noted in the official race report. Protests against a "racing incident" ruling are permitted but rarely succeed, as the stewards have broad discretion. The 2021 British GP (Verstappen/Hamilton) illustrates how the same incident can be interpreted differently: the stewards found Hamilton predominantly at fault (10s penalty) while Hamilton's team argued Verstappen was at equal fault. Both positions are supported by different readings of the guidelines.
