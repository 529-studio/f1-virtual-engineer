The Virtual Safety Car (VSC) was introduced in 2015 under Art 48 supplement. When deployed, all cars must reduce speed to a predetermined delta time displayed on the dashboard — effectively 30-40% slower than race pace. Cars must not exceed this delta time or they receive a penalty (Art 54). Critically, cars must not proceed at a speed that prevents another car from overtaking safely under VSC conditions. The pit entry speed limit applies from the moment VSC is deployed. VSC creates strategic opportunities: teams can pit during VSC with less time loss than a normal pit stop because all cars are slowed. The exact delta varies by circuit and is transmitted by the FIA system. A controversial aspect is the transition lap: on the VSC IN board, drivers must continue at delta speed until the green light — there have been cases where drivers accelerated slightly before the green flag, investigated as a VSC violation. Timing of the VSC deployment (e.g. being called out when a leading car has just pitted and rivals haven't) is often seen as favouring or disadvantaging certain teams, though the FIA insists deployment is purely safety-driven.
